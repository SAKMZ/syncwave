Run your own

Your server. Your music. Your rules.

There's no hosted service to sign up for. Syncwave runs on your machine and your friends connect to it. Setup takes about five minutes.

Desktop

Download and double-click

Unzip and run start.bat on Windows or start.sh on macOS and Linux. It fetches what it needs, opens your browser, and prints a public link you can send to anyone.

Linux server

One command

Installs everything on Ubuntu or Debian. Give it a domain and it sets up HTTPS too.

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SAKMZ/syncwave/main/scripts/install.sh | sudo bash

Hosting on a cloud VPS? YouTube refuses most datacenter IPs, so you'll need a proxy pool or a cookies file. It's one setting in the admin console, and the self-host guide walks through it.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Does everyone need Spotify, or Premium?

No. Nobody needs an account with anything. Your server fetches the audio and streams it, so listeners just open a link.

How many people can join a room?

There's no built-in cap. The real limit is your upload speed, since each listener streams from your machine. A group of friends on a home connection is comfortable.

Can I use it alongside Discord?

That's the most common setup: voice in Discord, music in a Syncwave room. Unlike a music bot, everyone hears full quality and can queue, vote and see what's playing.

Do I have to pay for the AI DJ?

No. Run it locally with Ollama and no key at all, or use a free Gemini key from Google. It stays off until you turn it on, and your key never leaves your server.

Where does the music come from?

YouTube Music, fetched with yt-dlp and cached on your disk. Syncwave is a self-host tool for you and your friends. Please don't run it as a public or paid service.

Is it free?

Free to run, fork and change for personal, hobby and nonprofit use under the PolyForm Noncommercial license. Selling access or bundling it into a paid product needs a separate agreement, so get in touch if that's you.
